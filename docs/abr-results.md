Opening on the lowest rung cut median startup from 6.1 s to about 4.0 s compared with hls.js’s built-in ABR, and the difference matches the extra bytes in a 720p first segment. No algorithm rebuffered on a 30-second clip. BOLA reached the highest average bitrate, but by spending its buffer on a rung the link can’t sustain: 720p averages ~2.4 Mbps against ~2.15 delivered. hls.js tried 720p and backed off after one segment, and the throughput algorithm never climbed.

slow4g (~1.2 Mbps measured). The link delivers less than even the lowest rung needs (~1.36 Mbps with audio), so every algorithm stalled about 7% of the time. The algorithm only changed startup: hls.js’s 720p opening took ~3.9 s longer for one segment of 720p. On this network the fix is a lower rung, not a different algorithm.

At ~3.1–3.5 Mbps measured, 720p was sustainable. hls.js used it for almost the whole session with no rebuffering, at the cost of about 1 s more startup than opening at 480p. The throughput algorithm stayed at 480p throughout, as predicted, because it needs an estimate of about 4,250 kbps before it will choose 720p. BOLA’s results are inconclusive: one session took 22.7 s to start, and its average bitrate was lower than at 2,500k, which needs investigating before it can be interpreted.
